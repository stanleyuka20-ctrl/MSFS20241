# Research notes: RMS Titanic, 14-15 April 1912

**Status tags:** [DOC] means a cited source supports it. [RECON] means it is a plausible reconstruction. [FICTION] means it is a game invention.

**Verification caveat:** Direct page fetches were blocked by the sandbox. Facts were checked against search-engine summaries of the cited pages, accessed 2026-10-03. The British Wreck Commissioner's and US Senate inquiry transcripts (titanicinquiry.org) are primary sources and should be read directly before scripting.

## 1. Documented timeline (ship's time)

All items are [DOC].

| Time | Event | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 10 April 1912 | Maiden voyage departs Southampton, calling at Cherbourg and then Queenstown (Cobh) | S1, S2 |
| 14 April, from 6 p.m. | Weather perfectly clear and fine, no moon, stars out, not a cloud. Air temperature falls sharply, to 33 °F at about 7:30 p.m. and later 32 °F | S3 |
| **About 11:40 p.m.** | Titanic strikes an iceberg | S1, S4 |
| About 12:15 a.m., 15 April | Captain Smith orders the distress call. Phillips sends "CQD MGY" and the position; "SOS" is also used later | S5 |
| About 12:40-12:45 a.m. | First lifeboat lowered, probably No. 7 on the starboard side. Exact times are debated | S6, S7 |

| About 4:00-4:10 a.m. | The Cunard liner *Carpathia* begins picking up lifeboats and takes aboard about 705-712 survivors | S4, S8 |

- [DOC] People on board after Queenstown: about 2,224-2,228. Deaths: about 1,500. The 20 lifeboats had a total capacity of about 1,178. Water temperature was about −2 °C. (S1, S2)

## 2. Environment and conditions

- [DOC] The sea was a flat calm with no swell. With no breaking water at its base, the iceberg was harder to see. There was no moon, the stars were brilliant, and the air was freezing. (S3)
- [RECON] Visual and audio design:
  - starfield reflected on a black, glassy sea;
  - breath visible in the air;
  - very loud steam venting from the funnels after the engines stopped (widely reported in testimony; confirm with S3);
  - increasing list and bow-down trim;
  - deck lights staying on until near the end.

## 3. Ship architecture cues

- [DOC] The first-class **Grand Staircase** ran from the Boat Deck down to E Deck. It was about 60 ft high and 16 ft wide, lit by an iron-and-glass dome, with a carved panel and a clock showing "Honour and Glory crowning Time". (S9)
- [DOC] The **first-class dining saloon** on D (Saloon) Deck was 114 ft long and the full 92 ft width of the ship, in Jacobean style with white moulded plaster. (S9)
- [DOC] The **third-class dining saloon** was in two compartments, 100 ft long, seating 473, finished in white enamel. The **third-class general room** was aft, about 36 x 38 ft, panelled in pine and finished in white enamel. (S9)
- [RECON] Class barriers: gates and stair arrangements kept the classes apart under US immigration rules. Whether gates were locked during the sinking is disputed, so do not depict deliberate lock-ins as fact.

## 4. Everyday life aboard

- [DOC] Food differed by class. Third class ate plain, hearty meals in shared saloons, while first class dined on multi-course menus. (S10)
- [RECON] Third-class passengers were largely emigrants from Britain, Ireland, Scandinavia, the Middle East and elsewhere, in multilingual groups. Use Encyclopedia Titanica passenger biographies for authentic but fictionalised composites, never real identities.

## 5. Sensitive-content guidance

- Real named victims and survivors (about 2,200 people) have living descendants. Do not use real passengers as playable characters or NPCs with invented dialogue. Composite fictional passengers are fine.
- Do not depict people in the water dying of cold in close-up. Use distance, sound and darkness, and a cut away.
- Avoid myths presented as fact:
  - that the band's last tune was certainly "Nearer, My God, to Thee";
  - that third class were deliberately locked below;
  - that Titanic was the "first ship to use SOS" (an earlier use, by *Slavonia* in 1909, is widely reported; verify before saying anything);
  - "unsinkable" claims attributed to White Star.
- The *Californian* controversy should be stated neutrally, if at all.

## 6. Fiction boundaries

- [FICTION] Player missions such as reuniting a family or returning a lost item. The number of lives saved overall must not change: the player cannot add lifeboat seats or survivors beyond documented totals.

## 7. Open questions

- Lifeboat-by-lifeboat launch times and occupancy. These are contested; use Encyclopedia Titanica's "Lifeboats, Launch Times, List and Trim" research (S7) and choose one consistent model.
- Lighting and colour of third-class corridors (reports of green tiles; forum-sourced only).
- The exact time the ship's lights failed, and exact break-up geometry (forensic debate).

# Research notes: Berlin, April-May 1945

**Status tags:** [DOC] means a cited source supports it. [RECON] means it is a plausible reconstruction. [FICTION] means it is a game invention.

**Verification caveat:** Direct page fetches were blocked by the sandbox. Facts were checked against search-engine summaries of the cited pages (IWM, Bundesarchiv, NAM, GHDI and others), accessed 2026-10-03. ## 1. Documented timeline

All items are [DOC].

| Date | Event | Source |
|---|---|---|
| From August 1940 | Berlin is bombed by Allied air raids; large areas already lie in ruins by 1945 | S5 |
| **16 April 1945** | The Soviet offensive opens at the Seelow Heights, the last major German defensive line east of Berlin | S1 |
| Late April | Soviet forces encircle the city and fight towards the centre. Volkssturm (older men) and Hitler Youth are thrown into the defence, and tank traps and ditches are dug | S1, S6 |
| **30 April** | Hitler kills himself in the Chancellery bunker. The Red Army begins attacking the Reichstag | S1 |
| **2 May** | General Helmuth Weidling orders the Berlin garrison to surrender and hands the city to Soviet forces under Marshal Zhukov. The Reichstag is occupied | S1, S2 |
| 8 May | Unconditional German surrender is signed in Berlin-Karlshorst; it takes effect 8/9 May | S3 |
| 15 May | A five-tier Soviet ration-card system is introduced | S5 |
| From late May | Women aged 15-65 are conscripted to clear rubble (*Trümmerfrauen*), about 60,000 in Berlin | S5 (secondary; verify) |

## 2. Weather and time of day

- [RECON] Mid-to-late April 1945: spring, with trees coming into leaf among the ruins. Smoke and brick dust hang over the centre, fires burn at night and artillery flashes are visible on the horizon. **Exact daily weather is unverified.** Check Deutscher Wetterdienst (DWD) historical records before scripting.

## 3. Environment and architecture cues

- [DOC] **Flak towers** were massive concrete anti-aircraft towers (Zoo, Friedrichshain, Humboldthain) that also served as air-raid shelters for tens of thousands of people. (S5)
- [DOC] About 16 km² of rubble lay across Berlin. (S5, secondary)
- [RECON] Five-storey Wilhelmine tenement blocks (*Mietskaserne*) arranged around courtyards (*Hinterhöfe*), with façades sheared away to show the rooms inside. Cellars became shelters (*Luftschutzkeller*) linked by knocked-through walls. There are tram wires down, burnt-out vehicles, barricades of trams and rubble, white surrender sheets at windows after 2 May, and Soviet signposts in Cyrillic.

## 4. Everyday life

- [DOC] Civilians queued for hours for rations and fetched water from street hand-pumps between barrages. (S6, a secondary source quoting contemporary accounts; confirm against primary diaries)
- [DOC] The main daily struggles were hunger, finding water, keeping a roof overhead and keeping warm. German ration cards became invalid at the surrender. (S5)
- [DOC] Contemporary and retrospective accounts of women's survival are collected by German History in Documents and Images (GHDI). (S4)

## 5. Sensitive-content guidance (high sensitivity)

- **Sexual violence:** research estimates about 100,000 women raped by Soviet soldiers in Berlin (S5, S4). This must **never** be depicted or implied in gameplay. Acknowledge it only, if at all, in respectful documentary text, after consulting experts.
- **Suicides** (including families) were widespread. Do not depict them.
- **Nazi symbolism:** follow German law (StGB §86a) and platform rules. Show it only in historical context. No glorifying framing of the last defenders, Hitler Youth or the bunker.
- **Forced labourers, prisoners of war and surviving Jewish Berliners** in hiding were also in the city. Represent them with dignity, not as background.
- Do not treat the Soviet flag-raising on the Reichstag (a staged photograph) as a trophy moment.

## 6. Fiction boundaries

- [FICTION] All civilians, units and missions. No interaction with real figures (Hitler, Weidling, Zhukov). Never place the player in the Führerbunker.

## 7. Open questions

- The chapter's exact date. Before 2 May means street fighting; after it means occupation and aftermath. A post-surrender aftermath setting is recommended as more appropriate for a non-combatant witness.
- Daily weather (DWD records).
- Primary sources for water pumps and queues. Read the GHDI documents (S4) directly.

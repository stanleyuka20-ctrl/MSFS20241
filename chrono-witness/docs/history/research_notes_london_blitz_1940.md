# Research notes: London Blitz, 1940

**Status tags:** [DOC] means a cited source supports it. [RECON] means it is a plausible reconstruction. [FICTION] means it is a game invention.

**Verification caveat:** Direct page fetches were blocked by the sandbox. Facts were checked against search-engine summaries of the cited IWM, National Archives, RAF Museum and London Fire Brigade pages, accessed 2026-10-03.

## 2. Weather and time of day

- [DOC] 7 September 1940 was a late-summer afternoon. Bombing began about 4 p.m. and fires lit the night sky over the East End. (S3, S5)
- [RECON] Warm, clear and sunny on 7 September, as widely recalled. The game should not rely on exact weather without checking Met Office Daily Weather Reports for 7 September 1940.
- [RECON] Night raids: searchlights, the bark of anti-aircraft guns (and falling shrapnel), the drone of unsynchronised German engines, an orange glow over the docks, smoke smell, and the blackout.

## 3. Environment and architecture cues

- [DOC] Docklands was densely populated and poor, with terraced housing next to warehouses, gasworks and docks. (S2)
- [DOC] Anderson shelter: two curved corrugated-steel sheets bolted at the top, sunk about 3 ft into the garden and covered with about 18 in of earth. (S2)
- [DOC] Tube shelters: the government at first discouraged them, then accepted them. Aldwych was converted into a shelter, and bunks, lighting and toilets were added over time. (S2)
- [DOC] Blackout: street lights and signs were turned off and vehicle headlamps masked. Wardens enforced "no lights". (S1)
- [RECON] Brick terraces with sash windows taped against blast, sandbagged public buildings, painted kerbs and lamp posts for the blackout, static-water tanks, and posters.

## 4. Everyday life

- [DOC] ARP wardens sounded sirens, guided people to shelters, reported damage and coordinated the other services. Fire-watcher rotas were organised among neighbours. (S1)
- [DOC] German bombers dropped a mix of high-explosive and incendiary bombs. (S1)
- [RECON] Ration books (rationing began January 1940), gas-mask boxes carried, tea from WVS mobile canteens, and the morning-after routine of glass, rubble and "business as usual" signs.

## 5. Sensitive-content guidance

- Civilian deaths, including children, were heavy. Depict rescue and heavy-rescue teams, shelters and community, and never show bodies in rubble close-up.
- Tube shelter disasters (Balham, October 1940; Bank, January 1941; Bethnal Green, 1943, which was a crush and not a bomb strike) must not become set-pieces.
- Avoid "Blitz spirit" myth-making only. Looting, anger at shelter provision and East End resentment are also documented. Present them fairly.

## 6. Fiction boundaries

- [FICTION] Named street, family and warden NPCs. Do not place real named firefighters or wardens in invented actions.

## 7. Open questions

- Which night the chapter uses (7 Sept, a later September night, or 29 Dec). This affects available kit: no Morrison shelters before 1941, and fire-watching compulsory only from late 1940.
- Exact weather for the chosen night (Met Office daily weather reports).
- Siren sound: the rising-and-falling "alert" tone and the steady "all clear". Get period recordings from IWM Sound.

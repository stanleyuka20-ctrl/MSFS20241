## 1. Documented timeline (local time, CET)

All items are [DOC].

| Time | Event | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 9 Nov, 6 p.m. | Politburo spokesman Günter Schabowski begins an international press conference, broadcast live on GDR television and radio | S1, S2 |
| Near the end of the press conference (about 7 p.m.) | Asked when the new travel regulation takes effect, Schabowski answers "sofort, unverzüglich" ("immediately, without delay"). A transcript and his handwritten note survive | S1, S3, S4 |
| Evening | Thousands of East Berliners gather at the border crossings | S2, S5 |
| **About 10:30 p.m.** (some sources say about 11:30) | **Bornholmer Straße** is the first crossing to raise its barriers. Crowds pour over the Bösebrücke into West Berlin. Other crossings follow during the night | S5, S2 |
| Night of 9/10 Nov | Crowds climb onto the Wall at the Brandenburg Gate. People celebrate on the Kurfürstendamm. West Berlin shops stay open late, and banks pay East Germans 100 DM "welcome money" | S6, S7 (news) |
| By 12 Nov | More than 3 million of the GDR's 16.6 million people have visited the West | S7 (news; verify) |

- [RECON] The time Bornholmer Straße opened varies between sources: barriers raised about 10:30 p.m. according to Berlin.de's monument listing, and full opening about 11:30 p.m. in other accounts. Choose one and present it as "late evening".

## 2. Background facts

- [DOC] The border system ran about 28 miles (45 km) through the city and about 75 miles (120 km) around West Berlin. (S8)
- [DOC] The "death strip" was a raked sand or gravel strip between the inner (hinterland) wall and the outer border wall. Concrete walls were up to about 5 m high, topped with barbed wire. The strip had watchtowers about 250 m apart, bright lighting at night and dog runs in places. (S8, S9, S10)
- [RECON] The final outer wall, "Grenzmauer 75", is commonly given as about 3.6 m high in L-shaped segments with a pipe on top. This was not verified in this pass; confirm with the Stiftung Berliner Mauer.
- [DOC] Checkpoint Charlie (Friedrichstraße and Zimmerstraße) was the crossing for foreigners and Allied personnel. On the eastern side were barrier poles, zig-zag concrete chicanes, a watchtower and a search area. (S11)
- [DOC] At least 140 people died at the Berlin Wall between 1961 and 1989. Chris Gueffroy, shot on 5 February 1989, was the last person shot dead while trying to escape. (S12, S13)

## 3. Weather and time of day

- [DOC/news] It was a cold November night. (S6)
- [RECON] Darkness from about 4:30 p.m. Single-digit Celsius temperatures. Sodium-orange West Berlin streetlights contrast with the white floodlights of the border strip. Breath visible in the cold. Trabant two-stroke exhaust smell. Check the DWD record for exact temperature and rain.

## 4. Environment and everyday-life cues

- [RECON]
  - **East Berlin:** Plattenbau panel blocks, worn pre-war façades, Trabants and Wartburgs, Intershop and HO stores, guards in GDR border-troop uniforms, people carrying ID cards (*Personalausweis*).
  - **West Berlin:** neon signs, the Kurfürstendamm, graffiti on the western face of the Wall (the eastern face was unpainted, whitewashed concrete).
  - **The night itself:** sparkling wine (*Sekt*), car horns, strangers embracing, "Wahnsinn!" ("Madness!") as a widely reported exclamation, and souvenir hunters with hammers chipping at the Wall (*Mauerspechte*) in the following days.

## 5. Sensitive-content guidance

- This is a joyful event, but it should acknowledge the Wall's victims (at least 140 dead), for example with a quiet moment at a memorial cross.
- Do not mock border guards. On the night, guards at Bornholmer Straße faced a dangerous decision, and the night passed without violence.
- Avoid the myth that one person "brought down the Wall". The opening came from mass protest, emigration pressure and a botched announcement.

## 6. Fiction boundaries

- [FICTION] All NPCs and missions (for example reuniting relatives or returning a lost item). Do not put words in the mouths of real people such as Schabowski or the Bornholmer Straße officer Harald Jäger. Do not alter the documented sequence of crossings.

## 1. Corrections to the premise

1. **The press-conference timing is right; keep it vague on screen.**
   - The press conference began at 6 p.m., live on GDR TV and radio. "Sofort, unverzüglich" came near the end, at about 7 p.m. [DOCUMENTED: `cdm_chronicle_1989`, `cdm_transcript`, `hdg_zettel`]
   - The planned 18:53-19:00 window matches the commonly cited timing, but the exact minute was not verified in this pass. Show "just before seven". [RECONSTRUCTION, `presser_minute`]
2. **Quote only "sofort, unverzüglich" as verified wording.** Paraphrase the rest of what Schabowski read (private trips on application, permits at short notice, emigration through all crossings) until it is checked against the transcript PDF. [RECONSTRUCTION, `presser_wording`]
3. **The opening time is disputed.** [DOCUMENTED: `berlin_de_bornholmer`]
   - Berlin.de's monument listing gives about 10:30 p.m. for the barriers going up. Other accounts give about 11:30 p.m. for the full opening.
   - About 23:30 for the gate fully opening is the usual figure and fine for gameplay. Documentary text should say "late evening" or "about 11:30 p.m.".
   - Earlier in the evening, the "valve solution" let some people out one at a time, with their ID stamped over the photo, which secretly cancelled their right to return. [RECONSTRUCTION, not verified in this pass, `valve_solution`]
4. **The neighbour cannot have a visa.** The rule was meant to start on 10 November, with a visa from a People's Police passport office. No one at the crossing that night had one. Her crossing depends on the gate opening, not on paperwork. [RECONSTRUCTION, not verified, `rule_intended`]
5. **Mostly on foot, not in cars.** At Bornholmer Straße people mostly walked across that night. Put Trabants and Wartburgs in the street and the side lanes, and queuing at crossings later. Do not show a stream of cars crossing at 23:30. [RECONSTRUCTION, `on_foot`]
6. **Bornholmer Straße is not Checkpoint Charlie.** Do not copy the zig-zag chicanes or the Allied hut. [DOCUMENTED: `sbm_checkpoint`, `britannica_checkpoint`]
7. **Brandenburg Gate was not a crossing.** People climbed the Wall there, but nobody crossed there that night. [DOCUMENTED: `deutschland_de_dancing`, `ghdi_wall_image`]

## 3. The crossing and the Wall

- [DOCUMENTED] The former crossing is now a listed Wall monument site. (`berlin_de_bornholmer`)
- [RECONSTRUCTION] The Bösebrücke is a steel bridge over railway lines, linking Prenzlauer Berg (East) with Wedding (West Berlin, French sector). (`bridge_location`)
- [RECONSTRUCTION] **Checkpoint art direction.** On the eastern side: car and pedestrian lanes, low flat-roofed control buildings and booths, red-and-white barrier poles, fences and tall floodlights. Check against Stiftung Berliner Mauer site plans. (`checkpoint_layout`)
- [RECONSTRUCTION] **Border-strip art direction.** The strip ran along the railway: hinterland wall facing East, signal fence, patrol road, lights, then the outer wall facing West. The exact layout at Bornholmer is unverified. (`border_at_bridge`)
- [DOCUMENTED] The system ran about 45 km through the city and about 120 km around West Berlin. (`britannica_wall`)
- [DOCUMENTED] The death strip had raked sand or gravel, watchtowers (about 250 m apart), bright night lighting and dog runs in places. Walls were up to about 5 m high, some with barbed wire. (`britannica_death_strip`, `sbm_berlin_wall`, `berlin_de_wall`, `britannica_wall`)
- [RECONSTRUCTION] The last outer wall type, "Grenzmauer 75", is usually given as about 3.6 m of L-shaped segments with a pipe on top. (`grenzmauer_75`)
- [RECONSTRUCTION] The western face was covered in graffiti in many places. The eastern face stayed bare. (`wall_faces`)

## 4. People, objects and atmosphere

- [RECONSTRUCTION] People carried the blue GDR identity card (Personalausweis), keys and maybe a bag. Many came in coats over house clothes. (`id_cards`, `what_people_carried`)
- [RECONSTRUCTION] Most had no West German money. The 100 DM welcome money (DOCUMENTED, `sec_ap_wptv`) filled that gap. (`money`)
- [RECONSTRUCTION] Trabants and Wartburgs, two-stroke exhaust smell. (`cars`)
- [RECONSTRUCTION] On the western side: cheering, clapping, slapping car roofs, sparkling wine, hugging strangers, "Wahnsinn!". People came from nearby Wedding pubs. (`wahnsinn`, `west_side_greeting`)
- [DOCUMENTED] It was a cold November night. (`deutschland_de_dancing`)
- [RECONSTRUCTION] Exact temperature and rain were not verified. Use a cold, mostly dry night with visible breath, and check Deutscher Wetterdienst records. (`weather_detail`)
- [RECONSTRUCTION, computed] Sunset was about 16:25 CET. A waxing gibbous moon, about 11 days old, was up in the evening. (`darkness`)
- [RECONSTRUCTION] White floodlights over the strip; orange sodium street lamps in the West; TV lights later in the night. (`lighting_contrast`)

## 6. Sensitive-content guidance

- **The Wall's victims.** At least 140 people died at the Berlin Wall between 1961 and 1989. The last person shot dead while escaping was Chris Gueffroy, on 5 February 1989, nine months before this night. (`sbm_victims`, `sbm_gueffroy`)
  - Give them a quiet moment, such as a memorial cross scan or a line from the neighbour.
  - Never make it a joke, a collectible or a "score".
  - Do not invent victims at Bornholmer Straße.
- **Border guards** are not villains or fools. They faced a frightening decision with no orders, and the night ended without violence. Fictional guards should be tense, confused and human.
- **The cancelled passports** of the "valve solution" people are a real injustice. Present it plainly, without melodrama.
- **No "one person brought down the Wall" story.** The opening came from mass protest, emigration pressure and a botched announcement.
- **Joy with care.** Celebrate the night, but avoid triumphalism about the East or its people.

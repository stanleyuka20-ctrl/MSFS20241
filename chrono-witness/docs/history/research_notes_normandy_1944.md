# Research notes: Normandy, June 1944

**Status tags:** [DOC] means a cited source supports it. [RECON] means it is a plausible reconstruction. [FICTION] means it is a game invention.

**Verification caveat:** Direct page fetches were blocked by the sandbox. Facts were checked against search-engine summaries of the cited IWM, NAM, Met Office, National Archives, US Army Center of Military History, Britannica and Mémorial de Caen pages, accessed 2026-10-03.

## 2. Weather and time of day

- [DOC] There was a narrow weather window after storms. Sunrise at Omaha was 05:58 and sunset 22:07 (double summer time). (S2, S5)
- [RECON] 6 June: overcast with broken cloud, a fresh north-westerly wind, choppy sea and seasick troops. In the British sector, wind-driven tides covered obstacles faster than expected. (S6)

## 3. Environment and architecture cues

- [DOC] **Bocage** begins about 10 miles inland: ancient hedgerow banks, half earth and half hedge, 1-4 ft thick and 3-5+ ft high, enclosing small fields, orchards and sunken lanes. (S9)
- [DOC] German defenders used old stone villages as strongpoints. (S7)
- [RECON] Norman farms are built of pale limestone (Caen stone), with half-timbered barns, slate or tile roofs, apple orchards and cider presses, stone churches with tall steeples (used as observation posts), and coastal villas behind the beaches. The beaches have steel "hedgehogs", ramps and stakes, concrete bunkers, and seawalls at some points.

## 4. Everyday life

- [RECON] For French civilians: occupation rationing, cellars as shelters during the bombardment, refugee columns on country roads, and liberation celebrations mixed with grief over civilian dead. Cows killed in fields were a widely reported sight; depict them at a distance.
- [RECON] For Allied soldiers: battledress, Mk II/III helmets (British/Canadian) or M1 helmets (US), compo rations, and cigarettes as currency.

## 5. Sensitive-content guidance

- Do not recreate the Omaha landing as a gore spectacle. Show fear, noise and the courage of medics and engineers, and keep casualties at a distance or implied.
- Represent the French civilian cost honestly (about 20,000 dead), including Allied bombing of towns such as Caen.
- Do not depict executions of prisoners or massacres (for example the Abbaye d'Ardenne murders of Canadian prisoners) as gameplay. If referenced, use documentary text only.

## 6. Fiction boundaries

- [FICTION] The player's unit or village and all NPCs. Real units (for example the 50th Division on Gold, 3rd Canadian on Juno, 1st and 29th US on Omaha) may be named as background but must not be credited with invented actions.

## 7. Open questions

- Which beach or inland location and which date the chapter uses. This determines the tide, H-Hour and nationality of the troops.
- Exact cloud and wind on 6 June at the chosen beach (Met Office archive, S2).
- Village destruction state by date. Allied bombing of Norman towns began on D-Day itself.

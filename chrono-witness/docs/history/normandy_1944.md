# Chapter research: Normandy, night and morning of 6 June 1944

The machine-readable version is `normandy_1944.json`. It uses the same fact IDs and source keys as this file. The older, broader note is `research_notes_normandy_1944.md`.

**Status tags:** **[DOCUMENTED]** means a cited source supports it. **[RECONSTRUCTION]** means it is plausible but not a specific recorded fact. **[FICTION]** means it is a game invention.

**Verification caveat.** Sources dated 2026-10-04 were checked in this pass. Sources dated 2026-10-03 come from the older note, whose author checked them the same way. The sandbox blocked direct page fetches for every reference host tried: history.army.mil, nationalww2museum.org, ibiblio.org, the Library of Congress and others. DOCUMENTED items were therefore checked against search-engine summaries of the cited pages, not full reads. ## 1. Corrections to the premise

1. **The cricket is a 101st Airborne item. Its use by the 82nd is disputed.** [DOCUMENTED: `eisenhower_clicker`, `amheritage_clicker`, `whn_cricket`]
   - The 101st ordered about 7,000 brass ACME clickers and issued them just before D-Day. The idea came from its commander, Maxwell Taylor.
   - The convention: **one click is answered by two clicks.**
   - The 101st's division history says only the 101st used it. Cornelius Ryan's *The Longest Day* describes 82nd men using it too.
   - The hamlet near Sainte-Mère-Église is in the 82nd's area. Scattered 101st men did land all over the peninsula, so make the lost paratrooper a 101st man who came down far from his zone. That keeps the cricket safe. [RECONSTRUCTION]
2. **"Flash" / "Thunder" is right.** The challenge was "Flash" and the answer "Thunder", valid for the first 24 hours. A third word, "Welcome", appears in later popular accounts but was not confirmed here, so leave it out or tag it. [DOCUMENTED: `eisenhower_clicker`, `amheritage_clicker`]
3. **The drop times differ by division. Use "after midnight", not one exact minute.** [DOCUMENTED: `nww2m_timeline`, `cmh_utah_airborne`, `aar_82nd`]
   - 00:15: 101st pathfinders.
   - About 00:48-01:40: 101st main drop. The official CMH history gives the start as "about 01:30".
   - 01:21: 82nd pathfinders.
   - 01:51: 82nd main body begins jumping.
   - 04:04: 82nd gliders begin landing.
4. **The night was moonlit, not pitch dark.** A nearly full moon was at its highest at about 01:19. Light the scene with moonlight broken by moving cloud. [DOCUMENTED: `skytel_dday`]
5. **No rain is recorded for the drop.** Cloud banks over the coast and a north-westerly wind are documented. Script wet grass and wind, not a downpour. [DOCUMENTED: `nww2m_airborne`, `cmh_xchannel_ch8`; rain is RECONSTRUCTION]
6. **"Before the naval bombardment begins" means before about 05:30.** [DOCUMENTED: `pritzker_utah_naval`, `nhhc_neptune`]
   - The Utah bombardment was due at 05:50, but ships opened fire early, at about 05:30-05:40.
   - B-26 bombers struck the beach at about 06:10, and H-Hour was 06:30.
   - The hamlet is about 10 km inland, so the family would hear and see the barrage on the horizon. It was not aimed at them. Earlier in the night there was also aircraft noise, flak and the Sainte-Mère-Église fire. [RECONSTRUCTION]
7. **Clocks.** All Allied times are British Double Summer Time (GMT+2). German clocks in France were also on GMT+2, so a French kitchen clock, a German watch and an American watch all show the same time. Sunrise at the hamlet is about 06:00 (computed). [DOCUMENTED: `iana_tz_uk`, `c1900s_time`, `cmh_omaha_ch3`; German side and hamlet sunrise are RECONSTRUCTION]
8. **An "Achtung Minen" sign is not proof of mines.** German boards with "Achtung Minen" are documented, and so are dummy minefields. Whether any particular inland field was mined is unknown, so keep the danger real but unconfirmed. [DOCUMENTED: `ttt_minefields`, `ttt_dummy_positions`]
9. **Arm flags were not universal.** Oilcloth 48-star flags are documented. Collector sources say mainly the 82nd wore them in Normandy. A 101st man without a flag is the safer default. [DOCUMENTED: `paratrooper_be_flags`, `qm_museum_aotm`; unit use is RECONSTRUCTION]
10. **The local building material is earth as much as stone.** The marshes around Carentan and the Merderet have France's densest concentration of earth-walled ("bauge") farms, with the cider press, cellar and stables under one roof. Use slate for most roofs and thatch on a few. [DOCUMENTED: `pnr_terre`; roof mix is RECONSTRUCTION]

- [DOCUMENTED] More than 18,000 Allied airborne troops were dropped that night. The American parachute force was six regiments, more than 13,000 men, in about 925 C-47s. (`iwm_dday_air`, `cmh_utah_airborne`)
- [DOCUMENTED] A cloud bank over the Cotentin broke up the formations, and flak scattered them further. Men landed far from their zones and gathered in small mixed groups. The scatter also confused the Germans. (`nww2m_airborne`, `cmh_utah_airborne`)

## 3. Terrain: floods, bocage, farms

- [DOCUMENTED] **Flooding.** The Germans flooded the Merderet and Douve valleys, partly by opening locks near Carentan. Grass showing above the water hid the floods from reconnaissance. Some paratroopers drowned under their equipment. (`historynet_lafiere`, `nww2m_airborne`, `cmh_utah_airborne`)
- [DOCUMENTED] A separate flooded belt lay behind Utah Beach, crossed by four causeways. The 101st was to seize their western ends. The Merderet crossings near Sainte-Mère-Église were at La Fière and Chef-du-Pont. (`cmh_utah_airborne`, `historynet_lafiere`)
- [DOCUMENTED] **Bocage:** small fields, orchards and sunken lanes enclosed by hedgerows on earth banks 1-4 ft thick and 3-5 ft or more high. (`doubler_bocage`)
- [DOCUMENTED] **Earth-walled marsh farms.** Example: the Maison des Marais at Marchésieux, built 1773, has the hearth room, stables, cellar, cider press, cart shed and hay lofts under one roof. (`pnr_terre`)
- [RECONSTRUCTION] Stone buildings (granite or schist further north) with steep slate roofs; thatch on some older cottages; cider-apple orchards grazed by cattle; barrels of cider and calvados in the cellar. (`wiki_norman_arch`)

## 4. German defences near the hamlet

- [DOCUMENTED] **Rommelspargel.** Rommel ordered fields suitable for gliders to be staked with poles about 4-5 m high, to be wired and mined. Over a million were put up. (`cmh_xchannel_ch7`, `wiki_rommelspargel`)
- [RECONSTRUCTION] Many staked fields were unfinished in June, with few mines or wires. Their effect on the landings was limited.
- [DOCUMENTED] **Mine signs.** German "Achtung Minen" boards and dummy minefields are documented. (`ttt_minefields`, `ttt_dummy_positions`)
- [RECONSTRUCTION] Flooded meadows, a hand-painted mine board at a hedge gate and a staked field are enough for the hamlet. Do not place bunkers or beach obstacles 10 km inland.

## 6. Civilians

- [DOCUMENTED] **Leaflets.** After French pressure, SHAEF agreed to drop warning leaflets before air attacks. They were headed "Message urgent" and told people to leave towns and roads. Some drifted off target, and German authorities blocked evacuation in some towns. (`historytoday_leaflets`, `eisenhower_scaef21`)
- [DOCUMENTED] About 20,000 Norman civilians were killed in the Battle of Normandy, nearly a third of all French civilians killed in the war. About 3,000 died on 6 June alone, mostly under Allied bombs, including about 700 in Lisieux. (`memorial_caen_battle`, `ap_civilians`, `afp_civilians`)
- [DOCUMENTED] In Sainte-Mère-Église, 25 civilians died on 6 June and 4 on 7 June. About 60% of the buildings were destroyed or damaged in the fighting of 6-7 June. (`dday_overlord_sme`)
- [RECONSTRUCTION] Rural families sheltered in cellars, ditches, quarries and orchard trenches, and many left with carts for farms further away. (`afp_civilians`)

## 7. Angoville-au-Plain: real event and what to borrow

- [DOCUMENTED] Pvt. Robert E. Wright and Pvt. Kenneth J. Moore were medics of the 501st Parachute Infantry, 101st Airborne. They turned the Angoville church into an aid station, hung a Red Cross flag and made everyone leave weapons outside. Over about 72 hours, under fire, they treated more than 80 wounded, including about a dozen Germans and a local child. Both received the Silver Star. Stained-glass windows honour them, and the pews are still bloodstained. (`healthmil_medics`, `bdn_moore`, `nwg_angoville`)
- [FICTION] **Safe to borrow:**
  - a stone parish church as an aid station;
  - a Red Cross flag;
  - a rule that weapons stay outside;
  - wounded of both sides treated side by side.
- **Do not borrow:**
  - the medics' names or their patient count;
  - the child patient;
  - the windows or pews;
  - the 72-hour story.
- Credit the real event in the journal.

## 9. Sensitive-content guidance

- **Civilians killed by Allied bombing.**
  - State the cost plainly in the journal: about 20,000 Norman civilians dead, about 3,000 on 6 June alone, mostly from Allied bombs.
  - Do not frame liberation as costless.
  - The farm family's fear of the bombardment is of *Allied* guns, and the game should not soften that.
  - No civilian bodies on screen. A damaged house or an empty chair is enough.
- **Prisoners.**
  - Both sides took prisoners that night. John Steele was captured at Sainte-Mère-Église (`army_sme_church`).
  - Any prisoner is shown being treated humanely: a German soldier sitting under guard, or a wounded German treated in the church.
  - Do not depict or reference the killing of prisoners as gameplay. If it is mentioned at all, use neutral documentary text only.
- **No glorified combat.**
  - The player is unarmed and never fights.
  - Gunfire, flak and shelling are heard and seen at a distance, as light, sound, shaking and dust.
  - No slow motion, no kill cams, no heroic music over violence.
- **No killing objectives.** No mission rewards harm to anyone. "Success" means someone is sheltered, found, guided or treated.
- **Wounds.** The wounded man has a bandaged leg or arm and shows pain and exhaustion. No gore.
- **Dead paratroopers and animals.** Drowned paratroopers in the floods are part of the record. Do not show them; mention them in the journal. Dead cattle, if shown at all, appear only at a distance.
- **Skippable events.** The naval shelling and the aircraft crash are skippable. A crashed C-47 or glider is shown as wreckage and fire at a distance, with no crew bodies and no rescue failure staged for drama.
- **The French family.** They are people with their own concerns (livestock, a missing son, the farm), not props for American heroism.

## 10. What the game must NOT claim

- That the hamlet, family, paratrooper, wounded man, church, French civilian or crash existed. All are FICTION.
- That the player affected any real operation, causeway, bridge or casualty figure.
- That every paratrooper used a cricket, or that the 82nd definitely did.
- A single exact drop time for "the paratroopers", or a stated weather or moon state beyond what is documented above.
- Bunkers, beach obstacles or Atlantic Wall casemates in the inland hamlet.
- The Angoville medics' names, numbers or details, or John Steele's steeple episode, recreated with fictional characters.
- That the "Achtung Minen" field definitely held live mines.
- That the "Flash / Thunder / Welcome" three-word form is confirmed.

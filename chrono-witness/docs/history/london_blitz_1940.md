## 1. Corrections to the premise

1. **The dawn signal is "Raiders Passed", not handbells.** The official signals were:
   - **Warning:** a warbling (rising and falling) siren, plus whistles.
   - **Raiders Passed:** a continuous steady siren. This is the colloquial "all clear".
   - **Gas:** rattles.
   - **Gas Clear:** handbells.
   - Use the steady siren at dawn. If the warden rings a bell, it means gas clear, which does not fit this scene. [DOCUMENTED: `tna_signals`, `iwm_warden_objects`]
2. **Fire-watching was voluntary in September 1940.**
   - Neighbours were encouraged to set up rotas.
   - The September 1940 Fire Watchers Order covered only large premises.
   - Compulsory fire-watching came in January 1941.
   - The player helping with sand and a stirrup pump as a volunteer is correct. [DOCUMENTED: `iwm_air_raid_life`, `ww2cd_fireguard`]
3. **Tube shelters had no bunks in September 1940.** [DOCUMENTED: `tfl_sheltering`, `ltm_shelter`, `iwm_blitz_factsheet`]
   - The government had just given way to crowds who bought tickets and stayed.
   - Expect crowded platforms, stairs and passages, little sanitation, and people bringing their own bedding.
   - Reservation tickets (30 November 1940), Tube medical posts (20 December 1940), chemical toilets, canteens and about 24,000 bunks all came later.
   - Do not show bunks, shelter tickets or "Tube Refreshment" trains.
4. **No Morrison shelters.** They were produced only from January 1941. [DOCUMENTED: `iwm_morrison`]
5. **Static water tanks (EWS) are an anachronism risk.** Most large tanks and flooded basements were built after mains failures in 1940-41, many in 1941-42. The burst main in the premise is good. A large stencilled EWS tank on the street should be dropped or kept low-key. [DOCUMENTED: `tate_ews`; risk assessment is RECONSTRUCTION]
6. **Say "ARP", not "Civil Defence".** The name became official only in 1941. [DOCUMENTED: `iwm_bravery`]
7. **Ration books, but no points coupons.** Points came later, from late 1941 (that date not verified here). [Ration timeline DOCUMENTED: `iwm_rationing`]
8. **Rescue "silence periods" are plausible but unsourced.** I found no IWM, TNA or LFB source describing Blitz listening periods, only tertiary or modern rescue doctrine. Keep the mechanic, but tag it RECONSTRUCTION. [`wiki_cds`]

## 2. Context

- [DOCUMENTED] The Blitz began on 7 September 1940, followed by 57 consecutive nights of bombing on London. It ran until May 1941, with over 43,500 civilians killed across Britain. (`iwm_blitz_factsheet`, `tna_blitz`)
- [DOCUMENTED] Bombers dropped a mix of high-explosive bombs and incendiaries. (`iwm_air_raid_life`)
- [DOCUMENTED] Docklands was densely populated and poor. (`iwm_blitz_factsheet`)
- [DOCUMENTED] About 120,000 people sheltered in the Tube on 21 September 1940. (`tfl_sheltering`)

## 4. Objects and environment

- [DOCUMENTED] **Incendiaries.** The 1 kg B1E "Elektron" was a magnesium-alloy case filled with thermite. It did not explode but burned hot enough to melt steel. They were tackled with a Redhill scoop and sand container, and with stirrup pumps worked by a team of two or three. (`iwm_b1e`, `iwm_redhill_scoop`, `iwm_stirrup_pump`, `iwm_incendiary_poster`)
- [DOCUMENTED] **Under the stairs.** The 1938 Home Office booklet *The Protection of Your Home Against Air Raids* advised a refuge room. Early advice suggested sheltering under the stairs. (`booklet_1938`)
- [DOCUMENTED] **Anderson shelters.** Corrugated steel, sunk 3 ft and covered with 18 in of earth. Free to low-income families with a garden; over 2.3 million by September 1940; cold, damp and prone to flooding. Many East Enders without gardens used public shelters, railway arches or the Tube. (`iwm_blitz_factsheet`, `tna_anderson`, `tna_public_shelters`)
- [DOCUMENTED] **Blackout.** No visible light; masked headlamps; black-and-white kerbs; white bands on lamp-posts; gas-detecting paint on pillar boxes and lamp posts. (`tna_blackout`)
- [DOCUMENTED] **Gas masks** in cardboard boxes were issued from 1938, with "Take your gas mask everywhere" posters. [RECONSTRUCTION] By late 1940 many people had stopped carrying them. (`iwm_gasmask_c1`, `iwm_gasmask_poster`)
- [DOCUMENTED] **Window tape** against flying glass. (`iwm_1940s_house`)
- [DOCUMENTED] **Rationing.** Books issued November 1939; bacon, ham, butter and sugar from 8 January 1940; meat from March; tea from July (2 oz a week). (`iwm_rationing`, `tna_ration_book`)
- [DOCUMENTED] **Terraces.** 19th-century yellow London stock brick, slate roofs, sash windows. (`hist_england_poplar`, `hist_england_maria`)
- [RECONSTRUCTION] **Level layout:** front doors on the pavement, a back yard with an outside WC, a back alley, a narrow hall with the stair cupboard underneath, chimney pots.
- [DOCUMENTED] **"Business as Usual"** notices on bomb-damaged shops. (`iwm_business_usual`)

## 5. Night conditions

- [RECONSTRUCTION, computed] Full moon about 16 September 1940, last quarter about 23-24 September, new moon about 1 October. On a late-September night, a waning crescent rises after midnight, so the evening is dark and lit by fires, searchlights and gun flashes. Check against HM Nautical Almanac Office data.

## 7. What the game must NOT claim

- That the street, warden, family, neighbour, rescue party or First Aid Post existed. All are FICTION.
- That a real named station suffered a real incident involving the player. Do not recreate Balham (October 1940), Bank (January 1941) or Bethnal Green (1943).
- Bunks, reservation tickets, Morrison shelters, points coupons, compulsory fire-watching or "Civil Defence" branding in September 1940.
- A specific fare for the shelterers' ticket, or a specific weather or moon state stated as fact.
- That handbells mean the all clear.
- That gas was used, or that everyone carried gas masks.
- Specific casualty figures for the fictional street or night.
- The "Blitz spirit" as the only story. Shelter conditions caused real anger in the East End. [`tna_public_shelters`]

## 8. Sensitive-content guidance

- Show no bodies or graphic injuries. The elderly neighbour is dusty, frightened and bruised. Rescue tension comes from creaking timbers, gas smell, dust and darkness.
- Children are kept safe on screen. Their fear is shown through the mother's reassurance.
- The neighbour's deafness (he cannot hear calls during the silence) should be portrayed with dignity. The player could use tapping or light instead, which is a RECONSTRUCTION mechanic.

# Research notes: Pompeii, AD 79

**Status tags:** [DOC] means a cited source supports it. [RECON] means it is a plausible reconstruction. [FICTION] means it is a game invention.

**Verification caveat:** Direct page fetches were blocked by the sandbox (pompeiisites.org, britannica.com and others). Facts were checked against search-engine summaries of the cited pages, accessed 2026-10-03.

## 1. Date: a premise decision is needed

- [DOC] The traditional date, **24 August AD 79**, comes from manuscript copies of Pliny the Younger's two letters to Tacitus, written about 25-30 years after the eruption. (S4, S5)
- [DOC] In 2018 the Archaeological Park of Pompeii announced a **charcoal inscription** in a house in Regio V that was being refurbished (the "House with the Garden"). It reads "XVI K NOV", the 16th day before the Kalends of November, which is **17 October**. Fragile charcoal would not survive long, so the Park considers it highly probable the inscription dates from October 79, about a week before an eruption on about **24 October**. (S1)
- [DOC] Supporting evidence for autumn: finds of autumn fruit, and braziers in use. (S2)
- **Recommendation:** use **late October AD 79** as the working date, and have in-game text say "AD 79 (traditionally 24 August; recent evidence suggests October)". Do not state either date as certain.

## 2. Eruption timeline

All items are [DOC] unless marked [RECON]. Approximate times are local solar time.

| Phase | What happened | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Background, AD 62 | A violent earthquake damaged the Vesuvius area. Repairs were still incomplete in 79, and building sites are an authentic detail | S6 |
| Day 1, about 1 p.m. | Vesuvius erupts. Pliny describes a cloud shaped like an umbrella pine | S2, S5 |
| Day 1 afternoon to night (Plinian phase, about 18-20 h) | Pumice (lapilli) falls on Pompeii, which lay downwind to the south-east, at about 15 cm an hour, eventually up to about 2.8 m deep. Roofs begin collapsing within hours, and the first deaths come from collapsing roofs and floors | S2 |
| Night / early Day 2 | The eruption column collapses. A series of pyroclastic density currents (surges S1-S6) follow, of increasing power | S2, S3 |
| Day 2 morning | A pyroclastic surge reaches Pompeii and causes most of the deaths there. Pliny the Elder dies at Stabiae | S2, S3, S5 |

- [RECON] Day 1 light changes from normal daylight to an orange-grey murk under the plume. Falling pumice is light, grey-white and stone-sized, and rattles on tile roofs. By late afternoon it feels like night. There are earth tremors throughout.

## 3. Environment and architecture

- [DOC] The city walls are about 3 km round and enclose about 66 hectares. The population is estimated at 10,000-20,000. (S7)
- [DOC] Public buildings cluster around the **Forum** (south-west), the **Triangular Forum** (south) and the **Amphitheatre and Palaestra** (east). The great houses (*domus*) line the **Via dell'Abbondanza**. (S7)
- [DOC] A *domus* had an **atrium** (with its rain basin, the *impluvium*) and a **peristyle** garden surrounded by reception rooms, bedrooms and dining rooms. (S8)
- [DOC] Streets were paved in lava blocks, with **raised stepping stones** because the streets doubled as drains. (S9)
- [DOC] There were 150+ **thermopolia** (street food bars) with L-shaped counters holding embedded *dolia* jars. **Bakeries** such as that of Popidius Priscus had lava millstones and a large oven. **Fulleries** such as that of Stephanus cleaned and finished cloth. (S9)
- [DOC] Walls carried painted **electoral notices** (about 200 survive) and graffiti advertising shows and leaving personal messages. (S9)
- [RECON] Colour palette: red, ochre and black frescoes; shop fronts with wooden shutters; carts restricted on narrow streets; public fountains on street corners; a smell of fish sauce (garum) near the shops.

## 4. Everyday life

- [DOC] Pompeii was a mixed society of free citizens, freedpeople and enslaved people. Houses were often combined with shops. (S9)
- [RECON] An October setting: the grape harvest just finished, new wine fermenting in dolia, braziers lit indoors in cooler evenings, cloaks worn.

## 5. Sensitive-content guidance

- The **plaster casts** (Fiorelli's technique, from 1863) contain real human remains. Museums and the Park now stress treating them as people, not statues, and ICOM guidance favours clear signage and an opt-out. (S10, S11)
- Do not use cast poses as set-dressing, jump-scares or collectible scans.
- The deadly surge must not be playable spectacle. Recommended: the player is withdrawn during the pumice fall (Day 1 evening). The surge is told afterwards through a calm epilogue grounded in archaeology.
- Do not depict the forensic "boiling blood or exploding skulls" hypotheses (S12). They are contested and gratuitous.
- Enslaved characters must be depicted with dignity and agency, not as props.

## 6. Fiction boundaries

- [FICTION] All named townspeople and missions.
- Do not attach invented stories to real excavated individuals or named houses' real owners (for example the Vettii or Julia Felix) without labelling them as fiction.

## 7. Open questions

- Which date the design team adopts (see section 1).
- Wind direction and plume dispersal on Day 1, and exact pumice depth at the chosen location. Get this from volcanological literature (Sigurdsson et al.).
- Which buildings were still under repair or construction in 79. Recent Park excavations in Regio IX describe a construction site (S13).

# Research notes: Hiroshima, 6 August 1945

**Status tags:** [DOC] means a cited source supports it. [RECON] means it is a plausible reconstruction. [FICTION] means it is a game invention.

**Verification caveat:** Direct page fetches were blocked by the sandbox network policy, including hpmmuseum.jp, city.hiroshima.lg.jp and pcf.city.hiroshima.jp. Facts were checked against search-engine summaries of the cited official pages, accessed 2026-10-03. Confirm all wording on the live pages before shipping. Wherever possible, use the Hiroshima Peace Memorial Museum (HPMM) and the City of Hiroshima as the authority.

## 1. A note on tone (read first)

This chapter depicts the killing of about 140,000 people by the end of 1945 [DOC: S2]. Many survivors (hibakusha) are still alive, and their families live in the city today.

- The chapter is an act of witness, not a set-piece.
- The player must not be able to "save" anyone in a way that implies the documented outcome could be changed.
- The player is never framed as the person responsible for the bombing.

## 2. Documented timeline (all times Japan time)

All items in this table are [DOC].

| Time | Event | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Before dawn | Weather reconnaissance B-29s take off from Tinian for Hiroshima, Kokura and Nagasaki. Three more B-29s follow later: the *Enola Gay* carrying the bomb, a scientific observation plane and a photography plane | S3, S4 |
| 7:09 a.m. | Yellow air-raid alert, caused by the weather B-29 over the city | S3, S4 |
| 7:31 a.m. | All clear. People finish a simple wartime breakfast and set off for work. Mobilised students and volunteer corps gather at building-demolition sites | S3, S5 |
| Shortly before 8:15 | The weather plane has reported clear skies over the primary target. The *Enola Gay* and two observation planes approach | S3, S4 |
| **8:15 a.m.** | The bomb is dropped from about 9,600 m and detonates about **600 m above Shima Hospital**, the hypocentre. The aiming point was the T-shaped **Aioi Bridge**. The bomb burst about 160 m south-east of the **Hiroshima Prefectural Industrial Promotion Hall**, now the Genbaku (A-Bomb) Dome | S1, S4, S6, S7, S8 |
| About 9 a.m. to 3 p.m. | **"Black rain"** (rain carrying soot, dust and radioactive fallout) falls mainly to the north-west. It begins about 30-45 minutes after the explosion, is most widespread around 10 a.m. and stops about 3 p.m. | S9 |
| Rest of the day | Fires join into a city-wide conflagration. Relief starts immediately: the Army Marine HQ (the "Akatsuki Corps") at Ujina, which was relatively undamaged, fights fires and carries and treats the injured without waiting for orders | S1, S10 |
| 9 August | Streetcars run again between Koi and Tenma-cho (Nishi-Tenma), powered from a substation about 15 km west. Survivors without money ride free | S10, S11 |
| End of December 1945 | About 140,000 dead, out of a population of about 350,000. That figure includes residents, soldiers, people mobilised from surrounding towns, and people from Korea, Taiwan and mainland China | S2 |

[RECON] Japan time is one hour behind Tinian time. US mission documents often give Tinian times, so do not mix them in game text.

## 3. Weather and time of day

- [DOC] The morning of 6 August was mostly clear with limited cloud. The weather plane reported good conditions over the primary target, which was the deciding factor. (S3, S12)
- [RECON] It was a hot, humid midsummer morning. Light falls from low in the east-south-east at 8 a.m. and cicadas are audible. Exact temperature and humidity are unverified (see open questions).

## 4. The pre-bombing city

- [DOC] **Nakajima-hon-machi** (now the Peace Memorial Park) had been the city's commercial and cultural centre. The old Saigoku-kaido road (Nakajima Hondori) ran through it, lined with cinemas, cafés, restaurants and taverns. The city's first cinema opened there. From the gate of Jisenji Temple to the Aioi Bridge, the street had bell-shaped "lily of the valley" street lamps. (S7, S13)
- [DOC] The Aioi Bridge was rebuilt in 1932-34 with an arm to the tip of Nakajima, giving it its unusual T shape, and it carried streetcar tracks. That shape is why it was chosen as the aiming point. (S7)
- [DOC] About 160 retailers were trading on the **Hondori** shopping street in August 1945. (S13; confirm on page)
- [DOC] **Building demolition for firebreaks** began in November 1944. On 6 August, large-scale demolition was under way in several areas, including what is now Peace Boulevard. The work was done by:
  - students of about 12-14, from the upper grades of elementary school and the first and second years of junior high school;
  - volunteer citizen corps of men in their 40s and women.
  - Of about 8,400 students at demolition sites, about 6,300 died. (S5)
- [DOC] The Industrial Promotion Hall was designed by Czech architect Jan Letzel and completed in April 1915. It was 25 m high, had a stone-and-mortar exterior and a five-storey round tower with an oval dome. (S8, S14)
- [RECON] Wartime austerity: many shops shut or half-empty, monpe work trousers for women, national-civilian uniform for men, cloth air-raid hoods, wooden houses with clay-tile roofs, and narrow lanes along the seven river channels of the delta.

## 5. What survivors described, by distance

- [DOC] **Flash and sound.** Survivors called it *pika-don*: *pika*, a blinding flash, followed by *don*, a huge boom. (S15; widely documented)
- [DOC] **Heat** (City of Hiroshima, S1):
  - The fireball reached over 200 m radius within one second.
  - Ground temperatures near the hypocentre reached 3,000-4,000 °C.
  - Heat rays acted for about three seconds.
  - Exposed skin was burned up to about 3.5 km away.
  - Roof tiles within about 600 m melted and blistered.
  - Poles and trees were charred within about 3 km.
- [DOC] **Blast.** Nearly everything within about 2 km was destroyed by blast and heat together. (S1)
- [DOC] **The cloud seen from afar:**
  - About 6 km away, Seiso Yamada photographed the cloud about two minutes after the explosion and remembered it as "blood red". (S16)
  - At Kure, about 20 km away, a man at the Naval Arsenal photographed a huge cloud swirling up in the western sky.
  - About 30 km north (Kake-cho), witnesses saw it "glinting brilliant silver" in the sun.
  - It was visible across the prefecture and into Shimane.
  - Research suggests it reached about 16 km high. (S16, S17, S18)
- [DOC] **Black rain** fell to the north-west for several hours. (S9)

## 6. Depiction guidance (mandatory)

1. **No graphic injury close-ups.** Do not depict burns, skin, eyes, shadows of victims, or bodies at close range. Convey harm through survivors' words (sourced testimony, with permission and attribution) and through absence: an empty school bag, a stopped clock, a lunch box. Use museum objects as reference only with the museum's permission.
2. **No spectacle.**
   - Do not show the detonation as a cinematic explosion from the bomber's point of view or as a slow-motion fireball.
   - Recommended: cut to white, then silence, then sound returning.
   - Alternatively, place the player far away (Kure, or the hills about 20 km out) and show the distant cloud only, which is documented.
   - Never let the cloud be "beautiful" in framing or music.
3. **The player cannot be at the hypocentre** at 8:15. The time-travel device should withdraw the player beforehand, or the chapter should be set before and after.
4. **Aftermath scenes** should focus on relief: the Akatsuki Corps, rescue stations, and the first streetcar on 9 August with its free rides. They must not focus on suffering.
5. **No gameplay mechanics** (timers, scores, collectibles) tied to casualties or the moment of detonation.
6. **Children.** Mobilised students can be depicted alive before 8:15. Their fate is told only through memorial text, not shown.
7. **Consultation.** Consult HPMM, the City of Hiroshima and hibakusha organisations on content and wording, and do not use real victims' names without permission.

## 7. Fiction boundaries

- [FICTION] Every NPC and mission (for example "return a lost item", "deliver a message").
- Do not invent named victims at real sites such as Shima Hospital or specific schools.
- Do not claim anyone could have been warned.

## 8. Open questions

- The 8:15 a.m. temperature and humidity in Hiroshima on 6 August 1945, from Japan Meteorological Agency (JMA) or Hiroshima Local Meteorological Observatory records.
- Streetcar routes and car liveries in summer 1945, and which lines were running at 8:15. Ask the Hiroden archive.
- The exact Hondori retailer count and its source (S13).
- Permission and rights for any survivor testimony quoted in-game.
